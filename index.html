Microstructure · Interfaces · Mechanics

Understanding how metals transform.

We investigate how grain boundaries move, how defects shape microstructure, and how mechanical and thermal histories govern the properties of metallic materials.

Research themes

From atomic interfaces to bulk microstructures.

Our work connects fundamental mechanisms at grain boundaries with the evolution of real materials. We combine controlled deformation and annealing with advanced microscopy and diffraction to understand when, where, and why microstructures change.

01 / INTERFACES

Grain-boundary migration

Disconnection-mediated motion, shear coupling, migration under mechanical constraints, and the transition between thermally assisted and mechanically driven processes.

GBMDisconnections
02 / MICROSTRUCTURES

Abnormal grain growth

How local stresses, stored elastic strains, texture, and boundary character influence selective grain growth and microstructural stability.

Severe plastic deformation

Microstructure refinement, heterostructures, cyclic deformation, fatigue, and the mechanical response of ultrafine-grained and nanostructured metals.

Advanced microscopy & diffraction

Research lineage

A continuous story of deformation and interfaces.

The group's research builds on Oliver Renk's work on severe plastic deformation, grain refinement, fatigue, and mechanically induced boundary motion, and extends these ideas toward direct observation of grain-boundary dynamics and stress-coupled microstructure evolution.

Foundations — Oliver Renk

Earlier work explored microstructure evolution in heavily deformed metals, grain-boundary motion as a restoration mechanism, texture effects, and the transition from thermally assisted to mechanically driven migration. This foundation informs current questions about how boundaries respond to local mechanical conditions.

Oliver Renk · publication profile ↗

Current directions — Jing Tang & collaborators

Recent work combines AFM and electron microscopy to reveal shear-coupled grain growth, while ongoing studies examine abnormal grain growth, elastic strain fields, annealing twins, and grain-boundary mechanisms across length scales.
